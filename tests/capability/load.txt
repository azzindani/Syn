para word:solar-memo.docx:body . The estate generated 9,835,518 kWh over the period. Bearspaw Water Treatment Plant produced 3,082,638 kWh of that total, 31.3 per cent, and more than the eight smallest sites combined. Any conversation about estate performance is, to a first approximation, a conversation about that one site.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . Output is strongly seasonal. Summer generation reaches 4,040,022 kWh against 607,481 kWh in winter, a factor of 6.7. At the calendar-month level the swing is sharper still: July delivers 1,444,872 kWh and December 93,541 kWh, a factor of 15. Generation peaks at 13:00, which accounts for 1,300,197 kWh across the period.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . Two caveats bound every year-on-year comparison in this report. 2015 and 2023 are partial years: the fleet was still being commissioned in the first and the data ends part-way through the last. The strongest complete year was 2020 at 1,765,072 kWh. Reading counts also differ by site, from 317 to 31,590, so totals are not like-for-like and mean hourly output is the fairer ranking.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . Three things follow. First, the winter trough is steeper than irradiance alone explains, which points at snow cover on the arrays; clearing after heavy snowfall is probably the cheapest marginal kWh available to the estate. Second, the smallest sites generate so little that their metering and maintenance overhead deserves review on its own terms. Third, the concentration of output in one site is a single point of failure worth planning around.

para word:solar-memo.docx:body Heading_1 Method and assumptions
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . Every figure in this report derives from the 258,423 hourly readings on the data sheet. Nothing has been transcribed by hand. The scorecard columns are live Excel formulas -- SUMIF, AVERAGEIF, MEDIAN over an array condition, PERCENTILE.INC, MAXIFS and COUNTIF -- so refreshing the underlying readings updates the whole report chain without re-deriving anything.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . Year, month, hour and season are derived columns on the data sheet rather than computed inside each aggregate. That choice matters at this row count: a SUMIFS against a materialised month column is dramatically faster than a SUMPRODUCT that recomputes MONTH() over a quarter of a million dates for every row of output. The seasonal assignment follows the meteorological convention, with December grouped into winter alongside January and February rather than split across two years.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . Medians and percentiles are computed with array conditions rather than with helper columns, which keeps the scorecard self-contained but makes those two columns the most expensive to recalculate. If the workbook is extended to substantially more rows, they are the first candidates for a helper-column rewrite.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . Percentages of estate output are computed against a named range covering the whole kWh column, so the share column sums to exactly one by construction rather than by coincidence. Where this report quotes a share to one decimal place, the underlying formula carries full precision.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . Two conventions are worth stating because they change the numbers. Zero readings, which dominate the overnight hours, are retained rather than filtered: removing them would raise every mean in the scorecard by roughly the proportion of dark hours and make cross-site comparison meaningless. And totals are reported as metered, without any normalisation for installed capacity, because capacity figures are not present in this dataset. Where capacity matters to the interpretation it is flagged in the text rather than silently corrected for.

para word:solar-memo.docx:body . The dataset holds 258,423 hourly readings across 91 distinct year-months. Coverage is not uniform. Sites were commissioned at different dates, so reading counts range from 317 to 31,590. Ranking sites by total output therefore rewards the ones that have been metered longest, and the mean and median columns on the scorecard exist to correct for that.
wtable word:solar-memo.docx:body . Year|Total kWh|Note;2015|36,570|partial;2016|166,262|complete;2017|1,143,066|complete;2018|1,714,572|complete;2019|1,725,009|complete;2020|1,765,072|complete;2021|1,603,815|complete;2022|1,584,848|complete;2023|96,304|partial
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . 2015 and 2023 are partial and must not be read as a trend. The apparent decline at the end of the series is an artefact of the data ending mid-year, not a fall in performance. Excluding both, the strongest year was 2020 at 1,765,072 kWh.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . Readings are hourly and appear to be meter totals rather than instantaneous power, which is the right basis for the energy figures used throughout this report. No negative values were observed. Zero readings overnight are expected and have been retained rather than filtered, because removing them would inflate every mean in the scorecard.

para word:solar-memo.docx:body Heading_1 Recommendations
para word:solar-memo.docx:body Heading_2 Clear snow from the arrays after heavy snowfall
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . The winter trough is steeper than irradiance explains. If snow cover accounts for even a third of the gap between December and a seasonally adjusted expectation, clearing is the cheapest marginal generation available to the estate. Start with Bearspaw Water Treatment Plant, where the same proportional gain is worth the most in absolute terms.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body Heading_2 Rank sites on mean hourly output, not on totals
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . Reading counts differ by up to a third across the estate, so totals compare commissioning dates as much as they compare performance. The scorecard already carries mean and median columns; committee reporting should lead with those.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body Heading_2 Review the smallest sites on their own terms
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . The four smallest sites together generate less than 4 per cent of estate output. That does not make them failures, but it does mean their metering, maintenance and reporting overhead should be judged against a much smaller benefit than the rest of the fleet.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body Heading_2 Plan around the concentration risk
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . 31 per cent of estate output comes from a single site. An outage there is not a rounding error in the annual figures, and the estate has no other site large enough to absorb it. This is worth an explicit contingency rather than an implicit one.
para word:solar-memo.docx:body Heading_2 Re-baseline once a full year is available
para word:solar-memo.docx:body . With 2015 and 2023 both partial, 2020 remains the only sensible baseline. Once a further complete year is in hand, the year-on-year comparison in this report should be redrawn against it.
